# Separate body and view rig

A first-person character has a **Body** Animator on the player root and a separate **View Rig** Animator under the player camera. The body owns the visible torso, pelvis, and legs and plays locomotion. The view rig owns the arms, weapon socket, and weapon meshes and plays the first-person animation. The camera remains a direct child of the player and copies the body head position; its rotation drives the child view rig around the camera pivot. An exposed local view-rig offset aligns each character's arms and weapon with that pivot. The head mesh can cast shadows without rendering inside the camera.

Nested Animator ownership is a general engine rule: each Animator evaluates only skinned meshes in its own subtree up to another Animator. This supports independent character parts beyond the AK. [`SplitFirstPersonController.cs`](../tools/templates/SplitFirstPersonController.cs) is an example controller script. Its body/view/camera references, input asset and action names, locomotion parameter names, speeds, and pitch limits are configurable. It sets locomotion parameters on the body and rotates the camera; the view rig inherits that rotation through the scene hierarchy. The engine has no AK-specific movement or camera behavior.

The AK installer uses the existing mesh sections in the source FBX: Head and Body remain under the body rig, Arms move under the view rig, and the AK weapon stays on the view rig's weapon socket. Its offsets are setup values for this asset, not engine constants. `Prepare-SplitFirstPersonTest.ps1` copies only the source assets needed into `build/` and installs the split scene there, leaving the source project untouched.

For controller authoring, the next graph editor should follow Godot's [AnimationTree blend graph](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html): typed pose pins, an Output node, searchable bone filters, and named rig controls. Each Animator can have its own graph. The view graph would evaluate `FP clip → Copy Gun Marker → Weapon Offset → Copy Grip Targets → Two Bone IK → Output`; the body graph evaluates locomotion independently. Controller serialization and runtime evaluation need to support that order before replacing the current rig asset editor.
